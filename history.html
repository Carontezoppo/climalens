The vortex seen from above the pole

A strong vortex is a tight, round ring of fast winds circling the pole. Before a sudden stratospheric warming it is pushed off the pole or pinched in two, and the ring of fast winds breaks up.

In midwinter the vortex core is typically −75 to −85 °C. Below −78 °C polar stratospheric clouds can form, which drive ozone loss. In a sudden stratospheric warming, air over the pole can warm by 30–50 °C within days.
